PepsiCo Seymour Distribution

"The trucks with the Pepsi logo lining up at the docks before sunrise — that's the operation." — the actual sight

Category: Distribution / Beverage logistics · 1001 N Sandy Creek Dr, Seymour, IN 47274 · Operated as part of PepsiCo's North America Beverages distribution network

Quick take. The Sandy Creek Drive facility is PepsiCo's regional beverage distribution operation for southern Indiana — warehousing and route delivery for Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Gatorade, Aquafina, and the rest of the PepsiCo beverage portfolio into stores, restaurants, schools, and vending across the territory. Solid mid-tier logistics employer; demanding route work, decent pay and benefits.

Who they are

PepsiCo, Inc. (NASDAQ: PEP) is one of the largest food and beverage companies in the world, with roughly $90B+ in annual revenue and a portfolio that includes the Pepsi-Cola Company beverages, Frito-Lay, Quaker, and Tropicana businesses. PepsiCo Beverages North America (PBNA) operates a network of company-owned bottling and distribution facilities across the U.S., supplemented by independent bottlers in some territories.

The Seymour facility is a beverage distribution operation that runs the warehouse-to-route function: trailers come in from PepsiCo bottling plants, product gets staged, route trucks are loaded for daily delivery to retail and food-service customers. The facility employs warehouse staff, route delivery sales representatives (the people in the Pepsi trucks at your local store), merchandisers (who stock the shelves on store visits), and management.

What they do well

Pay and benefits relative to entry-level work in town. Route delivery and warehouse positions at PepsiCo pay above retail and food-service entry-level. Benefits include medical, dental, 401(k) with match, and PTO. The Pepsi job is a real career-grade entry into corporate America for someone without a college degree.

Tenure and route stability. Route sales reps in particular tend to stay in their territories for years, building relationships with the store managers and food-service customers on their routes. That continuity is valuable for both the worker and the customer base.

Internal mobility. PepsiCo is known industry-wide for promoting from within. Warehouse workers move into routes; route reps move into territory management; territory managers move into broader sales and operations roles. This is documented in PepsiCo's annual sustainability and workforce reporting.

The training program. PepsiCo route-sales training is industry-recognized. The "delivery sales representative" role is a real sales job (managing customer orders, shelf placement, promotional displays) and PepsiCo invests in training the people who do it.

What people complain about

Physical demand. Route delivery means moving product cases — sometimes hundreds of cases a day per route. Climbing in and out of trailers, hand-trucking cases into stores, restocking shelves. Reviews on Indeed and Glassdoor consistently cite the physical demand as the hardest part of the job.

Long hours. Routes run until they're done. A route that runs into trouble — truck issue, store delays, weather — means a longer day, and routes can stretch to 10-12 hours. Reviews flag this.

Sales pressure on route reps. PepsiCo route reps have sales targets — not just delivering product but also driving incremental display, promotion participation, and account growth. For a worker who wants pure delivery without sales pressure, this isn't that. Reviews split: people who like the sales element thrive; people who don't churn out.

Weekend and holiday volume. Beverages and snacks sell hardest around weekends and holidays, which is when route demand is highest. Standard for the industry but it shows up in reviews.

How they handle it

PepsiCo as a publicly-traded multinational has full corporate compliance, ethics hotline, HR escalation, and union infrastructure where applicable (some PepsiCo facilities are union, others are not; the labor environment varies by location and is documented in trade press). Worker grievances that escalate to corporate compliance are tracked. The company publishes diversity, equity, and inclusion reporting.

Local impact

PepsiCo Seymour is a meaningful logistics employer with estimated headcount in the low hundreds. The route operation feeds southern Indiana retail and food service, including Schneck Medical Center cafeteria, Seymour Community Schools food service, every grocery store in town, and most of the restaurants. Indirect economic effect (vehicle maintenance vendors, fuel purchases, building services) adds to local spend. As a corporate citizen Pepsi participates in school sports sponsorship and event sponsorship at a moderate level.

Bottom line

7 / 10

PepsiCo Seymour is a solid regional logistics employer with the brand, training, and benefits backing of one of the world's largest CPG companies. For workers who can take the physical demand and like the route-sales element, this is a real career. For workers looking for desk work or lower-physical-demand options, it's not the right fit. As a Seymour neighbor, Pepsi is a quiet but consistent piece of the local distribution and employment base.
