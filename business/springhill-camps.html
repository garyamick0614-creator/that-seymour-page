SpringHill Camps — Indiana

"A summer-camp footprint big enough to send buses out across the Midwest." — the actual operating model

Category: Camps / Christian nonprofit · 740 Hartford Pl, Seymour, IN 47274 · SpringHill Camps founded 1969 (Michigan); Indiana property serves the Midwest region

Quick take. SpringHill Camps is a large, accredited Christian summer camp organization headquartered in Evart, Michigan, with a major Indiana property in Seymour serving thousands of kids each year. Day camps, overnight camps, family camps, retreats. Parents who send kids to SpringHill tend to send them back for years. The Christian programming is core; non-Christian families send kids too, knowing what's on the program.

Who they are

SpringHill Camps is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit Christian camping organization founded in 1969 by Enoch and Lois Olson in Evart, Michigan. From a single Michigan property it has grown into a multi-state operation with permanent properties in Michigan and Indiana plus a substantial day-camp program that sets up at host churches across the Midwest each summer. Total kids served per summer is in the tens of thousands across all programs and properties.

The Indiana property at 740 Hartford Place in Seymour is a permanent overnight camp facility with cabins, dining hall, lake/pond, climbing/zip elements, sports facilities, and the rest of a modern overnight camp setup. It runs week-long overnight summer camp sessions for elementary through high-school age groups, plus family camps and shoulder-season retreat use by churches and youth groups.

SpringHill is accredited by the American Camp Association (ACA), which means a third-party audit of safety, staff training, programming, and facility standards. ACA accreditation is meaningful in the camping industry and not all camps have it.

What they do well

Programming. SpringHill runs a high-quality activity program — ropes courses, climbing, kayaking, archery, sports, arts — with tight scheduling and full days that keep kids engaged. Reviewers consistently call out the energy and enthusiasm of camp staff and the variety of activities.

Safety culture. ACA accreditation, criminal background checks on staff, defined ratios, and trained lifeguards and ropes-course operators are all standard for SpringHill. Parents who research camp safety find SpringHill's documentation transparent.

Faith content delivered as advertised. SpringHill is openly evangelical Christian. Programming includes worship, Bible teaching age-appropriately delivered, and small-group discussions. Families who agree with this content love it. Families who don't shouldn't be surprised by it. The website and registration materials are clear about what camp includes.

Repeat attendance. Many SpringHill kids attend year after year, and counselors are often former campers who've grown up through the program. That continuity is real and is part of the cultural appeal for families who buy in.

What people complain about

Cost. Overnight camp at SpringHill is in the multi-hundred-dollars-per-week range, in line with other accredited overnight camps but a real cost for many families. Scholarship/financial-aid programs are available and well-publicized; not every family qualifies.

Religious content for non-Christian families. Some reviews describe expecting "summer camp" and getting "Christian summer camp." This is on the parent for not reading the registration materials, not on SpringHill, but it shows up.

Counselor turnover. Like all summer camps, SpringHill staffs heavily with college-age counselors who turn over each season. Some weeks land better than others depending on staff cohort. Standard for the industry.

Bus/transport logistics. Camp dropoff and pickup days have logistics that some families describe as chaotic. Standard for high-volume camp operations.

How they handle it

SpringHill has a centralized customer-service organization out of Michigan headquarters that handles registration, scholarships, refunds for cancellations, and parent concerns. Documented refund and weather-cancellation policies are published. Safety incidents are reported per ACA standards.

Local impact

The Seymour SpringHill property is a year-round employer and a seasonal host to thousands of campers. Summer staffing brings dozens of college-age counselors into Seymour for the season, with secondary effects on local restaurants, gas stations, and grocery. The off-season retreat business brings in church groups and youth ministry events. As a Seymour property, SpringHill is one of the most distinctive non-industrial uses of land in the area — rare for a town of this size to host a full-scale accredited overnight camp.

Bottom line

8.5 / 10

For families who want a high-quality accredited Christian overnight camp experience for their kids, SpringHill Indiana is one of the strongest options in the Midwest, full stop. For families who want secular summer camp, this isn't the camp — that's a feature, not a bug, and it's openly disclosed. As a Seymour neighbor, SpringHill has been a quiet anchor on the east side of town for years and is one of the things that distinguishes Seymour's portfolio of community institutions from nearby comparable towns.
