Silgan Plastics — Seymour Plant

"The bottle your shampoo or motor oil comes in might have been blown in Seymour." — the actual product line

Category: Manufacturing / Rigid plastic packaging · 201 S County Road 1100 E, Seymour, IN 47274 · Part of Silgan Holdings (NYSE: SLGN)

Quick take. Silgan Plastics' Seymour facility produces rigid plastic containers (bottles, jars, closures) for personal care, household cleaners, motor oils, and food clients. It's part of Silgan Holdings, a publicly-traded $5B+ revenue packaging company. Steady mid-pack manufacturing employer in town; not the highest-paying option but consistent, with the benefits structure of a publicly-traded U.S. industrial.

Who they are

The Seymour plant is one of multiple Silgan Plastics facilities operated by Silgan Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: SLGN). Silgan Holdings is publicly traded, headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut, with reported annual revenue typically above $5B. The company has three main segments: metal containers (the largest food-can producer in North America), closures, and dispensing/specialty plastics. The Seymour plant sits in the rigid plastic packaging organization — HDPE and PET bottles and jars produced via blow molding and injection molding, plus closures.

Customers are major consumer-products and industrial brands — the kind of names that sit on Walmart and Kroger shelves. Specific customer relationships are confidential under standard packaging-industry contracts but publicly Silgan supplies P&G, Unilever, Henkel, Church & Dwight, and the major motor oil brands among many others.

What they do well

Silgan Seymour delivers what most production-line workers in Indiana are looking for from a mid-tier industrial: a paycheck, a benefits package, predictable shifts in non-launch periods, and a job that doesn't go away when the seasons change. Health insurance, 401(k), and PTO are standard for a publicly-traded U.S. industrial. Skilled-trades work (process technicians, mold maintenance, electricians) pays meaningfully better than production and the plant runs internal training paths.

Plastic packaging is recession-resistant in a way automotive isn't — people keep buying shampoo and motor oil regardless of the business cycle — so Silgan Seymour layoff history is much milder than at the high-horsepower or automotive-tier plants. That stability is real value.

What people complain about

Wages relative to other Seymour plants. Silgan production wages are documented in Indeed reviews to sit below Cummins and the higher end of Aisin. For workers comparing options, this matters. Silgan's response — consistent across the industry — is that packaging is a lower-margin business than heavy diesel and automotive, which is true and doesn't help the worker's wallet.

Plant heat in summer. Blow-molding plastic operations run hot. Reviewers describe summer plant temperatures as a real challenge. The plant has cooling fans, hydration stations, and breaks; the underlying physics is the underlying physics.

Mandatory overtime around customer launches. When a CPG customer ramps up volume on a new SKU, the plant runs hot — literally and figuratively. 50+ hour weeks happen.

Promotion velocity. Reviews describe slower promotion paths than at some peer plants. This is partly a function of the long-tenure workforce — positions don't open up unless people leave or retire.

How they handle it

Silgan as a publicly-traded U.S. industrial has standard corporate compliance, ethics hotline, and HR escalation paths. Worker complaints that escalate to corporate are tracked. The company publishes sustainability reports including PCR (post-consumer recycled) plastic content targets and energy reduction metrics, which is increasingly important as CPG customers push their packaging suppliers on sustainability.

Local impact

Silgan Seymour employs in the low hundreds. Member of the Jackson County Industrial Development Corporation. Charitable contributions and event sponsorship at a moderate scale — not the corporate-anchor level of Cummins or Aisin but consistent. The plant is a steady piece of the east-side Seymour industrial corridor.

Bottom line

6.5 / 10

Silgan Seymour is a fine, stable mid-pack manufacturing employer. The pay is below the top tier, the work is hot and physical, but the job is steady, the benefits are real, and the company isn't going anywhere. For someone who wants a job they can keep for 20 years without worrying about layoff cycles tied to mining capex or vehicle program transitions, Silgan is a defensible pick.
