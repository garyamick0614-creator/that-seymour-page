Seymour Tubing, Inc.

"Precision welded steel tubing — for cars, trucks, furniture, fitness equipment, anywhere a hollow steel section is the right answer." — the actual product line

Category: Manufacturing / Steel tubing · 1140 N O'Brien St, Seymour, IN 47274 · Founded: 1956

Quick take. Seymour Tubing is a locally-headquartered specialty welded steel tubing manufacturer that's operated on N O'Brien Street since the late 1950s. Privately held, ISO-certified, supplies automotive, furniture, fitness, and industrial markets. Mid-size employer in town, less visible than Aisin or Cummins but a long-standing piece of the local industrial base.

Who they are

Seymour Tubing was founded in 1956 in Seymour, Indiana as a specialty welded steel tubing manufacturer. The plant on N O'Brien St runs steel coil-to-tube operations — high-frequency welded round, square, rectangular, and special-shape tubing produced in carbon steel and select alloy grades. The company is privately held and Seymour-headquartered, which puts it in the relatively small group of manufacturers that have stayed locally owned through six decades.

End markets are diversified: automotive structural and exhaust components, office and home furniture frames, fitness equipment frames, agricultural and material-handling applications, and various industrial OEM customers. The diversification is intentional — tubing demand from any one segment is cyclical, but multiple segments together smooth the cycle.

What they do well

Quality control on a specialty product. The customer base for precision welded tubing rewards consistency — out-of-spec tubing fails downstream fabrication, returns are expensive, and vendor relationships are sticky. Seymour Tubing has held ISO 9001 certification for years, which means an audited quality system that customers rely on.

Long tenure and local ownership. Workers describe a small-company culture relative to the multinational tier-1 plants in town. Decisions get made locally, the GM is approachable, and the business has been consistent enough that long-tenure employees are common. For a worker who values a smaller-company feel, this is a real differentiator from Aisin/Valeo/Cummins.

Stable customer base. Furniture and fitness markets are less volatile than the high-horsepower industrial cycle. Seymour Tubing has run more steadily through downturns than the highly cyclical local plants.

What people complain about

Wages below the top tier. Seymour Tubing pay is competitive for a mid-size privately-held manufacturer but doesn't match Cummins or top-end Aisin. This is consistent with the general pattern: small private specialty manufacturers pay less than large public industrials with bigger benefits budgets.

Limited promotion paths. Smaller company = fewer levels to promote into. Workers looking for management track or skilled-trades growth sometimes have to leave to advance.

Physical work in a hot mill. Tube mill operations are loud, hot in summer, and physically demanding. Standard for the steel industry but real.

Limited public review presence. Seymour Tubing has fewer Indeed and Glassdoor reviews than the bigger plants in town, which makes it harder to research as an applicant. The reviews that exist trend slightly positive on culture and slightly negative on pay relative to local options.

How they handle it

As a privately-held smaller company, Seymour Tubing operates closer to its workforce than a multinational does. Issues tend to get resolved at the supervisor or plant manager level rather than via corporate hotlines. The company has not been the subject of major OSHA actions, EPA violations, or labor disputes on public record — the regulatory profile is clean.

Local impact

Seymour Tubing is a steady local employer in the low hundreds. Member of the Jackson County Industrial Development Corporation. Less visible in event sponsorship than the corporate-anchor plants but a contributor to the local manufacturing tax base, vendor ecosystem (local trucking, maintenance, electrical contractors, etc.), and the secondary employment effects that mid-size private manufacturers generate. A locally-owned manufacturer that has stayed local for 70 years is itself a contribution that's hard to measure.

Bottom line

7 / 10

Seymour Tubing is a quietly important piece of the Seymour industrial base — the kind of mid-size private manufacturer that doesn't make headlines but employs hundreds of people for entire careers. If you're choosing between a smaller-company culture with modestly lower pay versus a multinational with higher pay and more bureaucracy, Seymour Tubing is the small-company option in town. For the right worker, it's the right job.
