Walmart Distribution Center #6088

"The reason there's a constant stream of trucks on US-50 east of town." — literally what's happening

Category: Distribution / Grocery logistics · 2725 W Tipton St, Seymour, IN 47274 · Opened: 2008

Quick take. Walmart Distribution Center 6088 is one of Walmart's grocery DCs, opened in 2008 to serve Walmart Supercenters and Neighborhood Markets across Indiana and the surrounding region. It runs 24/7, employs around 1,000 people, and is one of the few places in town where someone can start without a degree and earn a real wage with benefits within 90 days. It's also a hard, physical job and a high-turnover environment.

Who they are

DC 6088 is a Walmart-owned and operated grocery distribution center built in 2008 on the west side of Seymour off W Tipton St. It's a "perishables" / general grocery DC that pulls product from manufacturer suppliers, stages it, and trucks it to roughly 100+ Walmart stores in Indiana, parts of Illinois, Ohio, and Kentucky. The site footprint is over a million square feet of conditioned warehouse including freezer, cooler, and dry-goods sections. The Seymour DC is one of dozens like it that Walmart operates nationwide as part of its in-house logistics network.

Walmart Inc., the parent, is the largest private employer in the United States and one of the largest companies in the world by revenue. The Seymour DC is administratively part of Walmart's grocery supply chain organization out of Bentonville, Arkansas; the General Manager is the senior local leader.

What they do well

Three things that consistently come up in worker reviews and Walmart's own public materials:

Pay relative to other entry-level work in town. Starting hourly rates at DC 6088 are above local fast-food, retail, and many entry-level manufacturing roles. Walmart has raised DC starting wages multiple times in the last decade and shift premiums (overnight, weekend) are real. For someone who can't or doesn't want to start at Cummins or Aisin, this is the next-best income in Seymour.

Benefits from day 1 of employment for full-time associates: medical, dental, vision, 401(k) with match, paid time off accrual, life insurance, and the Walmart "Live Better U" tuition program (community college and select bachelor's programs paid for, with some restrictions). Eligibility timelines are clear and posted; this isn't a vague promise, it's documented.

Internal promotion paths to lead, area manager, GM, and corporate roles are publicly real. Walmart promotes from within; people who join as order pickers and stay can climb. The trade press and SEC filings document senior leaders who started in DCs.

What people complain about

The work is hard. Order picking grocery is a quota-driven physical job — you're moving cases, often in cooler or freezer sections, often at speed. Productivity expectations are tight and tracked electronically. Indeed reviews repeatedly mention shoulder, back, and knee strain, and high first-90-day attrition.

Mandatory overtime during peaks. Holiday seasons (especially Thanksgiving and Christmas) and weather events that disrupt store stocking generate forced 50-60 hour weeks with limited notice. Pay is time-and-a-half over 40 but the schedule wrecks plans.

Quota and write-up culture. Reviews describe a metrics-heavy environment where missed quotas can lead to written warnings and termination. Walmart is consistent about this across DCs nationally; whether that's "rigorous" or "punishing" depends on who you ask.

Workers' comp friction. Several public reviews and a small number of legal filings describe difficulty getting injuries acknowledged as work-related. Standard pattern in the warehousing industry; not unique to Walmart, but documented.

How they handle it

Walmart has a corporate ethics hotline and a chain of HR escalation that workers can use. The company publishes its associate handbook and policies. On safety, OSHA recordable rates for Walmart DCs vary by site; the Seymour DC has had occasional citations consistent with industry norms but no major safety scandals on public record. Schedule complaints typically don't get resolved at the DC level — they're a function of corporate volume planning — but local management is generally responsive on individual issues like leave-of-absence approvals.

Local impact

DC 6088 is consistently in Jackson County's top 5 employers. Roughly 1,000 associates work the DC plus drivers and contracted services. Walmart contributes to Seymour Community Schools and to local United Way and food bank campaigns. The DC is Seymour's single biggest source of truck traffic, which is its own community impact — positive (logistics jobs) and friction (US-50 corridor wear, noise complaints from nearby neighborhoods).

Bottom line

7 / 10

If you need a job this week and you can take the physical work, DC 6088 is a real option with real benefits. The pay band sits above retail/food service, below skilled manufacturing. Turnover is high for a reason — the work is hard — but the people who stay can build a career path inside the largest company in the country, with tuition paid. As a community partner, Walmart shows up. As a workplace, it's not for everyone.
