The Townhouse Cafe

"Daily specials on a chalkboard, lunch crowd from the courthouse, and a tenderloin that goes off the menu by 1 PM." — how regulars describe it

Category: American / Cafe · 115 W 2nd St, Seymour, IN 47274 · Independently owned downtown lunch spot

Quick take. The Townhouse is the small downtown cafe a block off Chestnut where the Jackson County courthouse crowd, downtown business owners, and a steady rotation of regulars eat lunch. About 4.4 stars across 70+ reviews. Daily specials, sandwiches, hand-breaded tenderloins, soups, comfort food. Lunch only or breakfast-and-lunch depending on the day. Cash and card both fine.

Who they are

The Townhouse Cafe is an independently-owned restaurant on W 2nd Street in downtown Seymour, in the small block between Chestnut and the courthouse square. It's been on this block under the Townhouse name for years; the building itself is older. Operating hours run breakfast and lunch (typical small-cafe schedule, generally weekday-leaning, closing mid-afternoon). Catering is offered for local offices and small events.

This is not a chain. There is no parent company. It's a small, local, working cafe.

What they do well

Daily specials. The chalkboard is the actual menu most days. Comfort-food rotations — meatloaf, country fried steak with mashed and gravy, chicken and noodles, ham and beans — with sides that change depending on what's been prepped that morning. Reviews consistently call out the specials as the reason to come.

The breaded tenderloin. Indiana standard, but the Townhouse's version specifically gets called out in Google reviews. Hand-breaded, properly pounded out, served on a bun with the standard fixings. It is a high-volume seller and it sells out on busy days, which is the strongest signal you can ask for.

Soup and salad lunch combo. The default office-lunch order. Soups change; the standards (beef vegetable, chili, broccoli cheese, chicken noodle) appear in rotation. Reviewers describe the soups as homemade-tasting, not from-a-bag, which in 2026 is something you can no longer take for granted.

The room and the welcome. Small dining room, not stylized, not designed by a brand consultant. Servers know the regulars. New customers get welcomed without fuss. This is the actual product.

What people complain about

Limited hours and days. Reviewers occasionally note arriving for dinner or weekend lunch only to find the cafe closed. The Townhouse is what it is — a weekday lunch room, primarily — and that's been consistent for years. Showing up at 5 PM Saturday isn't going to work.

Specials run out. If you come at 1:30 hoping for the meatloaf, the meatloaf may be gone. This is the small-cafe model: cook a fixed quantity, sell until done, send people home with sandwiches when the special is out. Some reviewers don't love it.

Cash-or-cramped logistics on busy days. A handful of reviews describe the dining room being uncomfortably full at peak lunch on courthouse days. Small room + popular food = predictable result.

How they handle it

Independently owned cafe with a small staff — complaints get handled at the table or at the counter, in real time, by the person who ran the day. There's no hotline, there's no corporate. Public review responses on Google are limited; the management doesn't appear to engage heavily online, which is fine for the format and matches the operating style.

Local impact

The Townhouse employs a small staff. Like Larrison's, the impact is foot traffic and downtown texture rather than payroll size. The cafe is part of why downtown Seymour has a working lunch economy — courthouse staff, lawyers, bank employees, county workers, downtown small business owners all eat somewhere, and the Townhouse takes a meaningful share of that demand. Lose the Townhouse and you lose a daily institution that's been part of the downtown rhythm for years.

Bottom line

8.5 / 10

If you work downtown or you're visiting and want to see what real Seymour lunch looks like, The Townhouse is the answer. The 4.4 across 70+ reviews matches what locals will tell you in person. The hours are limiting and the menu won't accommodate every diet, but for what it sets out to be — a small honest weekday lunch place in a small Indiana town — it's exceptional. Order whatever's chalked up.
