Larrison's Diner

"Two eggs, biscuits and gravy, hashbrowns, coffee, and you're out the door under ten bucks." — the standing order most regulars don't have to say out loud

Category: American / Diner · 200 S Chestnut St, Seymour, IN 47274 · In some form on this block since the mid-20th century

Quick take. Larrison's is a small old-school diner on Chestnut Street that does breakfast all day, burgers at lunch, and is one of the consistent positive Google review magnets in Seymour. It's not fancy. It's not trying to be. The food is honest, the staff knows the regulars, and you'll see at least one farmer at the counter with a paper cup of coffee no matter what time you walk in.

Who they are

Larrison's Diner has operated on Chestnut Street in downtown Seymour for decades under multiple owners and names — the building and the rough format have stayed the same: counter seating, booths, breakfast-and-lunch hours, an open kitchen window where you can hear the spatula on the flat-top. The current operation has built one of the better local Google review profiles in Seymour with consistent 4+ star averages on triple-digit review counts. They are a small, independently owned business; ownership details are not heavily publicized, and that's by design — this is a diner, not a brand.

Hours are breakfast-and-lunch (typical small-diner pattern, generally 6 AM-2 PM, varying by day). They're closed certain holidays. Cash and cards both accepted. No reservations — you walk in, you find a seat, you order.

What they do well

Three things, every public review:

Breakfast. Eggs the way you actually ordered them. Hashbrowns crisp on the bottom. Biscuits-and-gravy that is the local pricing benchmark for the dish and consistently delivers. Bacon that's actually crisp. The reason people keep coming back is that the breakfast is reliable across hundreds of visits, which is harder to do than it sounds.

Burgers and the lunch flat-top work. Hand-pattied, smashed on the grill, served on toasted buns. The burger plate with fries is the standard lunch. Daily lunch specials rotate — meatloaf, country fried steak, sometimes an open-faced roast beef — and they sell out when they sell out.

The room. Seymour has corporate-chain breakfast options on Tipton St (waffle places, fast food, the truck-stop tier). Larrison's is the local alternative: small, friendly, downtown, and unpretentious. Customers consistently mention "feels like home" or "you're a regular by your second visit" in reviews.

What people complain about

Wait times on Saturday and Sunday. Weekend breakfast is the reliable pinch point. The diner is small, the kitchen capacity is what it is, and the wait can be 20-30 minutes for a table. Reviews flag this; regulars know to come early or come on a weekday.

Service inconsistency. Like any small operation that depends on the staff present that day, service quality has natural variation. Most reviews are warm; a minority describe rushed or distracted service on busy mornings. Standard small-diner pattern.

Limited menu / hours. Larrison's is breakfast-and-lunch. If you want dinner, that's not what they do. If you want vegan, gluten-free, or specialty diet items, the menu accommodates only partially — this is meat-and-potatoes Indiana diner cooking.

How they handle it

This is a small independently-owned diner. They respond to issues at the counter, on the spot, by remaking a plate or fixing a check. There's no corporate complaint hotline because there's no corporate. That's the actual model.

Local impact

Larrison's employs a small staff (kitchen + servers, well under 20 people). The economic impact isn't payroll; it's foot traffic to downtown Seymour. The diner is part of why Chestnut Street has working morning energy, alongside Rails Craft, the Townhouse Cafe, and the other downtown anchors. A diner like this either anchors a small downtown or it doesn't exist; Larrison's anchors.

Bottom line

9 / 10

If you're new to Seymour, eat breakfast at Larrison's within your first month. If you've lived here for ten years and you don't go, you should. It's not the fanciest, biggest, or trendiest food in town. It's the most Seymour. The 4+ star Google rating across triple-digit reviews isn't an accident — it's the same plate, done right, year after year. That's a kind of business that's getting harder to find anywhere in America, and it still exists on Chestnut Street.
