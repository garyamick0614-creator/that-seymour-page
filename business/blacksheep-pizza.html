Blacksheep Pizza (Brownstown)

"Worth the 12-mile drive for a pizza." — the consensus from Seymour residents

Category: Pizza / Independent · 510 N Pine St, Brownstown, IN 47220 · Independently owned, Brownstown-based, with a Seymour-area regional pull

Quick take. Blacksheep Pizza is one of the highest-rated independent restaurants in the Seymour area — about 4.8 stars across 100+ Google reviews. It's in Brownstown (the next town over from Seymour, about 12-15 minutes west) and the reason it's on this Seymour list is that Seymour residents drive there. Hand-tossed crusts, real cheese, generous toppings, and the kind of small-shop discipline that makes a difference plate to plate.

Who they are

Blacksheep Pizza is an independently-owned pizza shop in Brownstown, IN, the Jackson County seat just west of Seymour. It opened in the 2010s and has built a regional following from Brownstown, Seymour, Crothersville, Vallonia, and the surrounding Jackson County footprint. Dine-in, carry-out, and local delivery. Hours run dinner-focused with a lunch window (check the current Google listing — small-shop hours can shift).

The operation is a single-location, family-run small business. There is no chain. There are no franchises. That matters for the consistency story below.

What they do well

The pizza itself. Hand-tossed dough that has actual chew and structure, sauce that tastes like tomato rather than corn syrup, mozzarella that's real cheese, and toppings that are applied generously and recognizably. Reviewers single out specific specialty pizzas (BBQ chicken, taco, supreme) and also rate the basic pepperoni at the level you want a basic pepperoni to be at — which is the test for a pizza shop.

Value. Pricing is competitive with chain delivery and substantially better than chain quality. Reviewers repeatedly mention the size-to-price ratio favorably.

Consistency. The reason Blacksheep has 4.8 stars is that the experience is consistent. Most independent pizza shops have a wider quality variance than the chains; Blacksheep doesn't. That's a craft-and-management achievement and it's the actual differentiator.

Small-business service. Reviews mention staff knowing regulars, taking custom orders without making it a problem, and handling carry-out logistics smoothly even at peak Friday-night volume.

What people complain about

Distance from Seymour. If you're a Seymour resident, Blacksheep means a 12-15 minute drive west to Brownstown. For a Friday-night carry-out, that's a real consideration, especially in winter. Reviewers from Seymour acknowledge this and still drive.

Wait times on busy nights. Like every popular pizza shop, Friday and Saturday peak hours can mean 45+ minute wait quotes for carry-out. The shop is small, the oven capacity is what it is, and the demand is real.

Limited menu breadth. Blacksheep is a pizza shop. It does pizza, breadsticks, salads, and a small set of sides — not a full Italian-American menu. If you want a sub or pasta or wings as the main, this isn't the place.

Phone busy at peak. Small shops with high call volume have phone-busy issues. Online ordering has helped but the phone problem persists at peak hours.

How they handle it

Blacksheep responds to public reviews on Google with consistency — the management actually engages, thanks customers, addresses negative feedback when warranted. That's an underrated quality signal. On the operational side they've added online ordering and what looks like an additional oven over time as volume has grown.

Local impact

Single-location small business employing a small staff. The economic impact isn't direct payroll size; it's the regional draw — people come from outside Brownstown specifically for this restaurant, which pulls foot traffic into a town that doesn't have many other regional draws. That's the kind of independent business that anchors a small downtown. Brownstown gets the direct benefit; Seymour gets the secondary benefit of having a great local pizza option 12 minutes away.

Bottom line

9 / 10

If you live in Seymour and you don't know Blacksheep, you should. The 4.8 across 100+ reviews is exceptional and matches the experience. Make the 15-minute drive once on a non-peak weekday evening, order a large pepperoni and one specialty, and decide for yourself. Most Seymour residents who try it once end up driving over a few times a month.
