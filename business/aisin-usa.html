Aisin USA Manufacturing

"The transmissions in your Camry probably rolled off a Seymour line." — not a slogan, just true

Category: Manufacturing / Automotive supplier · 1700 E 4th St, Seymour, IN 47274 · Founded (Seymour plant): 1989

Quick take. Aisin USA is the North American manufacturing arm of Aisin Corporation, the Japanese tier-1 automotive supplier majority-owned by Toyota Group. The Seymour plant is one of Aisin's flagship North American sites — transmissions, drivetrain, and body parts that ship into Toyota, Honda, GM, Ford, and Stellantis assembly lines. Steady, well-paying union-free manufacturing work; tough on the body but consistent.

Who they are

Aisin USA Manufacturing was incorporated in Indiana in 1989 as Aisin's first major U.S. production site, riding the late-1980s wave of Japanese tier-1 suppliers following Toyota into the American market. The Seymour location was chosen for its rail access, Interstate 65 proximity, and the labor pool stretching from Columbus to Bedford to Bloomington. Over 35+ years it has expanded multiple times, now operating across multiple buildings on the east side of town with a workforce in the high-1,000s.

The parent company, Aisin Corporation (formerly Aisin Seiki), is headquartered in Kariya, Japan and is part of the Toyota Group keiretsu. Toyota Motor Corporation is the largest single shareholder. Globally Aisin is one of the top 10 automotive suppliers in the world by revenue, with major product lines in transmissions (manual, automatic, CVT, hybrid e-Axle), brakes, body components, and increasingly EV components.

What they do well

For Seymour, Aisin's value is straightforward: it's a real career-grade manufacturing job. Production associates start at competitive Indiana automotive-supplier wages with shift differentials, healthcare from day 90, paid time off, and a 401(k) match. Skilled trades (maintenance technicians, tool-and-die, controls) earn substantially more, and Aisin runs an in-house apprenticeship and skill-up program that's well-regarded inside the plant.

The company is famous in automotive circles for its production discipline. Aisin runs the Toyota Production System (TPS) the way Toyota does — standard work, kaizen, andon cords, the whole vocabulary — and the Seymour plant is audited against those standards. For workers who care about doing things right, that's a draw. For workers who don't, it's an irritation. Both reactions are real.

Aisin invests in the community in visible ways. The plant has run STEM partnerships with Seymour Community Schools, has been a multi-year sponsor of the Jackson County United Way campaign, and Aisin associates volunteer hours at local charities through company-organized days of service.

What people complain about

The complaints are the standard automotive-tier complaints, and they're real:

Mandatory overtime. When automotive demand spikes, Aisin runs mandatory Saturdays and sometimes Sundays. Indeed reviews repeatedly mention being scheduled for 50-60 hour weeks during volume peaks. The pay is good but the schedule is hard on family life, and it can change with limited notice.

Physical demands. Production line work is repetitive motion, often heavy parts, often standing for the full shift. Workers report wrist, shoulder, and back issues. The plant has on-site athletic-training and ergonomics programs, but the underlying job is physical.

Temp-to-hire pipeline. Aisin uses staffing agencies (Kelly, Manpower, others over the years) for entry-level production. Temps work alongside direct hires for the same line task often at a lower pay band, with no benefits, and conversion to direct can take months. Recurring complaint, public and consistent across review sites.

Management variation. Glassdoor and Indeed have a wide spread on supervisor reviews — some teams are praised, others described as high-pressure. This is a 2,000-person factory; some variance is expected.

How they handle it

On safety, Aisin has historically maintained OSHA recordable rates better than industry averages and the plant has a visible safety program. On the temp pipeline, the company will tell you it's how they manage demand fluctuations and how they evaluate workers before direct hire; that's industry-standard reasoning, and it doesn't make the temps' experience easier. On overtime, federal Fair Labor Standards Act applies and Aisin pays time-and-a-half on hours over 40, which they do.

Local impact

Aisin is consistently in Jackson County's top three private employers, alongside Cummins/Walmart Distribution and Rose Acre. Estimated Seymour-plant headcount is roughly 1,800-2,200 depending on production volume. Direct payroll alone is a large fraction of Seymour's economic base; secondary effects (housing, retail, schools) trace back to Aisin paychecks. The company has been a long-running corporate sponsor of the Seymour Oktoberfest, contributes to Schneck Medical Center capital campaigns, and partners with Ivy Tech Columbus on workforce training.

Bottom line

7.5 / 10

If you want a stable, decent-paying manufacturing job in southern Indiana, Aisin USA is one of the better options on the board. Pay, benefits, and tenure stability are above what you'll get at a non-tier-1 supplier. The cost is the schedule, the physicality, and the temp-to-hire path. As a corporate citizen of Seymour, Aisin shows up for the community in concrete ways. As a place to spend 30 years on a line, it's not for everyone — but for the people it works for, it works for a long time.
