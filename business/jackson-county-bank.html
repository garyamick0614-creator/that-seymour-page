Jackson County Bank (JCB)

"The bank where the loan officer might know your dad." — not always literally true, but it's the brand promise

Category: Banking / Community bank · 125 S Chestnut St (main office), Seymour, IN 47274 · Founded: 1909 (originally Jackson County Loan and Trust)

Quick take. JCB is the locally-headquartered community bank that's been part of Seymour for over a century. Owned by holding company JCB Financial Corp, FDIC-insured, with branches across Jackson and surrounding counties. They've earned regional "Best Place to Work" recognition multiple times and customers who switch to JCB from a big regional bank rarely switch back.

Who they are

Jackson County Bank traces back to 1909 in Seymour. Today it operates as a wholly-owned subsidiary of JCB Financial Corp, the holding company. The bank has approximately a dozen branches concentrated in Jackson, Washington, Lawrence, and surrounding counties of southern Indiana, plus extensive ATM access. Total assets are in the hundreds of millions to over a billion (the exact figure tracks year by year and is reported on the FFIEC call reports).

Unlike Old National, First Financial, Chase, or Fifth Third — all of whom have Seymour branches — JCB is locally headquartered. Decisions about local lending happen in Seymour, not in a regional credit center in Cincinnati or Indianapolis. That's the practical difference customers feel.

What they do well

Local underwriting. Small-business loans, farm loans, and home mortgages get evaluated by people who know the local economy. A Jackson County farmer applying for an operating line of credit has a meaningfully better experience at JCB than at a national bank that doesn't underwrite to local agriculture realities. Same for a small contractor or main-street retailer.

Customer service in branch. Google reviews of the Chestnut St main office and outlying branches are consistently positive on the in-person experience. Tellers know regulars by name. Staff turnover is lower than at the chains, so you actually see the same people more than once.

Recognition as an employer. JCB has been named multiple years to "Best Places to Work in Indiana" by the Indiana Chamber of Commerce. That's a real, audited recognition, not a self-awarded badge. Employee tenure tracks the recognition — people stay.

Community involvement. JCB executives sit on local nonprofit boards (Schneck Foundation, Jackson County United Way, the Boys & Girls Club, the Chamber). Sponsorship dollars go into Seymour Oktoberfest, the Jackson County Fair, and local school programs. The bank's name shows up in concrete, repeated places.

What people complain about

Digital banking lag. Community banks across the country trail the megabanks in app and online-banking features, and JCB is no exception. Reviews mention occasional slowness or feature gaps in the mobile app. The bank uses third-party core software (typical for community banks), so feature pace depends partly on the vendor.

Branch hours and ATM coverage. Outside of Seymour and the immediate Jackson County footprint, ATM access is via shared networks, which can mean fees in some locations. National travelers occasionally complain about this. Within the local footprint, it's not an issue.

Loan denial complaints. Like any underwriter, JCB declines applications. A subset of public complaints comes from declined applicants, which is normal but worth flagging. There are no public CFPB or FDIC enforcement actions against JCB on record.

How they handle it

JCB has a customer service line and branch managers who handle escalations directly. The bank is small enough that escalating to a regional VP or even the CEO's office is realistic for serious issues. FDIC consumer complaint data shows minimal volume relative to the bank's size, which is a strong signal — you can verify this on the FFIEC complaint database.

Local impact

JCB is one of the largest locally-owned employers in Seymour with roughly 150-200 employees across the bank's footprint. Local lending kept Jackson County small businesses functional through the 2008 financial crisis (when national banks pulled back) and the 2020 COVID disruption (where JCB participated heavily in PPP). The economic-development effect of having a locally-headquartered bank with a community-oriented loan portfolio is hard to overstate; small towns that lose their community banks lose access to small-business credit, full stop. Seymour didn't lose its.

Bottom line

8.5 / 10

JCB does what a community bank is supposed to do: knows the local economy, lends to it, hires from it, and gives back to it. The trade-off versus a megabank is digital convenience for relationship banking. For most Seymour residents and small businesses that's a trade worth making. If you live here and bank elsewhere out of habit, switching to JCB is one of the higher-leverage local choices you can make.
